# Why Rent Well uses tabs and a drawer

I used both a bottom tab bar and a drawer, with a native stack around them, because a property management app has two very different kinds of places. Landlords and tenants each have a few things they open almost every day, so those became tabs: a landlord gets Home, Payments, Work Orders, and Messages, and a tenant gets My Home, Payments, Repairs, and Messages. Everything a small landlord only opens now and then, like Properties, Tenants, Leases, Expenses, the Mileage Log, Profit, Documents, Checklists, and Settings, lives in the drawer, grouped into short sections so the tab bar never gets crowded. The detail screens, like a single work order, a tenant's profile, a lease, a rent ledger, or a conversation, are stack screens that push over the tabs with their own header and back arrow, so drilling into something and coming back feels the same everywhere. Each role only gets its own tabs and drawer, and switching sides in the demo rebuilds the whole navigator on the other side's home. The navigation map in `navigation/navigationMap.js` says where every screen lives, and the tests and the diagram are built from it, so they always match what the app really does.

A few choices might need explaining. Every screen has exactly one header: the tab bar or the drawer draws it for the screens inside it with a menu button on the left, the stack draws it for detail screens, and the tests check that a second one never shows up. Payments is a tab instead of Tenants (which I planned last week) because checking who paid rent is the thing a landlord does most, and Tenants fits better next to Properties in the drawer. For back behavior, Android back from any tab goes to the home tab first, and back from a drawer section returns to the section you came from, while picking something from the menu starts fresh. When I added Leases and Profit later, they went into the drawer instead of becoming new tabs, and small forms like renewing a lease or sending a rent reminder open as sheets over the screen, so the tab bar stayed at four. Tenants don't have Documents or Checklists yet, so their drawer is short for now, and Settings and Property Overview are still simple stub screens that I plan to build out next.
